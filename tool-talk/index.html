Prompt, inspect, repair: an AI-generated counselling flow tested against our research

When an AI tool builds a flow from a short prompt, which assumptions about our users does it get wrong, and does a repair prompt fix them or just move the problem?

ProjectWhat Happens Next (D4)Shows students what happens after they contact a counselling service
AI toolClaude, claude-opus-5-5Fresh instance with no project files. Used in place of Figma Make
Runs1 prompt + 1 repair10 November 2026. Output kept unedited
BaselineTeam's D4 prototypeFlow designed from our IA, card sort and D3 concept
Workflow

What we did, in order

  1. Write a short prompt

    Three sentences, the kind a team types on day one. No research findings included.

  2. Generate v1

    6 screens, one HTML file.

    125 s · 34,491 bytes

  3. Inspect against our research

    8 requirements from D3/D4, plus an axe scan and scripted keyboard checks.

  4. Write a repair prompt

    Names the wrong assumption and the evidence against it. Asks for 3 changes.

  5. Generate v2

    Same conversation, new file.

    116 s · 37,957 bytes

  6. Re-inspect and compare

    Same 8 requirements, same scan, against the D4 flow.

What to watch for in this version
    Prompt that produced this version 
    
      
    
    
    
  
    

    


    
  
    
    The incorrect assumption
    
    v1 treated getting in touch as signing up
    
  
    
  
    
    
    The prompt asked for a flow that helps a student "understand what happens after they get in touch". It never said whether the student wants to book. The AI filled that gap with the most common pattern for service websites: a funnel that ends in a referral form.
    
  
    
  
    
    
    
      v1 · what the AI assumed
      Every route leads to the same first step: a short chat with a counsellor.
      
    The only action is a self-referral form. It requires an 8-digit student ID and asks what the student needs support with. Submitting it shows Thanks, Sam. Your referral has been sent. and a reference number such as NU-CS-4821, even though a note above the form says nothing is sent.
    
    
    
    
    
      D3/D4 · what our research found
      Ask the service a few questions. Not a booking.
      
    First-time students want to understand the process and ask questions before committing, and want to know they can stop at each stage. D4 puts a "can you stop here?" decision point on each stage, and the message it builds says I'm not booking yet.
    
    
    
  
    
  
    
    
    Two smaller assumptions came with it. v1 listed UK emergency numbers (999, Samaritans 116 123) and a campus security line as if they were real and verified for our users. It also stated service facts such as Within 2 working days with no sample label. The only disclaimer is one line in the footer.

    The axe row shows why the scan alone isn't enough. It rated v1 and v2 almost clean, yet R8 fails in both. Only stepping through the error case found it.
    

    


    
  
    
    Failure case
    
    The repair fixed what we named and invented what we didn't
    
  
    
  
    
    
    
      
    The repair prompt asked for 3 changes and got all 3. But to write "what stopping means" at each stage, the AI had to say how the service behaves. It doesn't know, so it made up policy and presented it as promises to a student:
    
      
      
        
    1. 01we won't contact you unless you've asked us something.
      2. 
        
    2. 02That's fine. We won't chase you. You can get back in touch whenever you're ready, even months later.
      3. 
        
    3. 03No appointment is made unless you ask for one.
      4. 
        
    4. 04you can … end the conversation early on the day. You don't need to give a reason.
      5. 
        
    5. 05The counsellor will explain how to come back later if you change your mind.
      6. 
        
    6. 06Stopping early won't affect whether you can use the service again.
      7. 
        
    7. 07If you email, you'll get an automatic reply saying your message has reached us.
      8. 
      
    
    
    
    
    
      
    Other side effects
    
      
      
        
    • One question is ticked before the student chooses anything. That nudges what they ask.
      • 
        
    • The invented address counselling@northbridge.ac.uk now appears in the copy instructions.
      • 
        
    • The funnel stayed. The repair changed sentences inside it and kept Step 2 of 6 and I'm ready to get in touch.
      • 
        
    • The error pattern from v1 carried over unchanged, because the prompt didn't mention it.
      • 
      
    
      
    What this means
    
      
    A repair prompt is a local patch. It fixes the sentences you point at. It doesn't change structure you didn't name, and it fills new gaps the same way it filled the first one. Every repair needs the same inspection as the first draft.

    Limits of this evidence
    
    What this test can and can't show
    
  
    
  
    
    
    
      
    Can show
    
      
      
        
    • What one AI tool assumed about this task when the prompt left the user's goal open.
      • 
        
    • That a targeted repair prompt fixed the named problems in one turn.
      • 
        
    • That the repair introduced new unverified content and didn't touch structure.
      • 
        
    • That an automated scan missed an error-recovery failure that a manual check found.
      • 
      
    
    
    
    
    
      
    Can't show
    
      
      
        
    • Other tools. We used Claude, not Figma Make. We haven't tested Figma Make's own claims.
      • 
        
    • Repeatability. One run each. AI output varies, so another run may make different assumptions.
      • 
        
    • A fair baseline. D4 meets 8 of 8 partly because our team wrote both D4 and the requirements.
      • 
        
    • An independent reviewer. A Claude session, from the same model family as the generator, ran the checks and drafted this audit. The team needs to repeat the walkthrough and confirm each finding.
      • 
        
    • Real accessibility. No screen reader was used and no disabled user tested any version.
      • 
        
    • Real users. Our requirements come from D3 research, but no student used v1, v2 or D4 for this test.
      • 
      
    
    
    
  
    
  
    
    
    Ethics. In a mental-health context, a confident invented sentence can do harm. v1's wrong emergency numbers and v2's invented promises ("Stopping early won't affect whether you can use the service again") would read as commitments from a real service. AI-generated content for this kind of service must stay labelled until someone checks it with the service.
    
  
    

    


    
  
    
    Recommendation
    
    When to use, adapt or avoid it
    
  
    
  
    
    
    Use for breadth
    Generate a first draft early, to surface topics and screen ideas the team missed. v1 raised appointment format and counsellor choice, which D4 doesn't cover.
    
    
    Adapt the prompt
    Put your research findings and content rules in the first prompt, not the repair. Inspect every version against written requirements, including error cases and keyboard use.
    
    
    Avoid for facts
    Don't take AI-written service facts, safety contacts or policy wording. Don't treat a polished flow as evidence about users.
    
  
    
  
    Course connection: this workflow fits D4 (fast alternative flows to compare against a chosen concept) and D5 (the R8 finding shows why an automated scan must be paired with manual checks).
