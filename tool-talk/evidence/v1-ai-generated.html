First time getting in touch?

Thinking about talking to someone? You don't need to have it all figured out.

Our counsellors support Northbridge students with anything that's affecting their wellbeing or studies. It's free, confidential, and separate from your academic record.

"I wasn't sure my problem was 'serious enough'."
There's no threshold. If it matters to you, it matters to us.
"How long until I actually talk to someone?"
Usually within 5 working days.

📚

Exam stress, perfectionism, procrastination, or falling behind.

🤝

Loss and change

Bereavement, homesickness, identity, or big life transitions.

Not on the list? That's fine. You can talk to us about anything.

Step 3 of 6

What happens after you get in touch

Here's the usual journey, so there are no surprises. Times are typical during term; they can be a little longer in exam periods.

  1. 1
    Straight away

    You'll get a confirmation

    An automatic email confirms we've received your referral, with your reference number and urgent help contacts.

  2. 2
    Within 2 working days

    We'll contact you to book a first conversation

    Someone from our team will contact you in the way you chose, to offer a time for an initial consultation.

    What if I miss the call?

    We'll try twice and then email you a link to book a slot yourself. Nothing is cancelled because you missed us.

  3. 3
    Within 5 working days

    Initial consultation (around 30 minutes)

    A relaxed conversation with a counsellor, by video, phone, or in person. You'll talk about what's going on and what you'd like to be different.

    Do I have to talk about everything?

    No. You decide what to share and how much. The counsellor won't push you.

  4. 4
    At the end of that conversation

    You'll agree a plan together

    This might be a set of counselling sessions, a workshop, a referral to another service, or simply knowing we're here if you need us.

  5. 5
    Usually within 2–3 weeks

    Your support begins

    If you go on to regular sessions, you'll be matched with a counsellor and given a regular time slot.

Step 4 of 6

Your first conversation with a counsellor

It's normal to feel nervous. Here's what it's actually like.

What usually happens

  • The counsellor introduces themselves and explains confidentiality.
  • They ask what's brought you here, in your own words.
  • They may ask some gentle questions about sleep, mood, and support around you.
  • You might fill in a short questionnaire (about 2 minutes) to help track how you're doing.
  • Together, you talk through what support might help.

Choose how you'd like to meet

Select one to see what to expect.

Things that might help

Before

Jot down a few notes on your phone if you're worried you'll forget something.

During

It's fine to pause, cry, say "I don't know", or ask the counsellor questions too.

After

Plan something gentle for afterwards. Talking can be tiring, even when it helps.

Step 5 of 6

Support you might be offered

There's no single route. After your first conversation, you and your counsellor will decide what fits best. You can change your mind at any point.

One-to-one counselling

Usually 4–6 weekly sessions, 50 minutes each

Regular sessions with the same counsellor to work through what's going on. In person, video, or phone.

Groups and workshops

Drop-in or 3-week courses

Examples this term: Managing Exam Anxiety, Sleep Better, and a weekly Grief and Loss support group.

Self-guided support

Start any time

Free access to an online CBT programme, with optional check-ins from a wellbeing practitioner every two weeks.

Sometimes another service is a better fit

If you'd benefit from specialist or longer-term help, we'll explain why and help you get connected, for example to:

Your GP

For medication reviews or NHS talking therapies.

Disability & Inclusion

For study adjustments such as extra exam time.

Student Advice Centre

For money, housing, or extenuating circumstances.

Step 6 of 6

Questions students often ask

Still unsure? These might help.

Will my lecturers or parents find out?

No. What you tell us is confidential and isn't shared with your department, family, or anyone else without your consent. The only exception is if we're seriously worried about your safety or someone else's, and even then we'd aim to talk to you first.

Will it go on my academic record?

No. Counselling records are kept separately and securely by the Wellbeing team. They aren't part of your student file.

Does it cost anything?

No. The service is free for all registered Northbridge students, including part-time, postgraduate, and distance learners.

Is my problem "serious enough"?

Yes. If something is affecting how you feel or how you're coping, that's reason enough. Many students come to us before things feel like a crisis, and that's a good thing.

Can I ask for a particular counsellor?

You can tell us if you'd prefer a counsellor of a particular gender, or someone with experience of a specific issue. We'll do our best to match you.

What if I change my mind after getting in touch?

That's completely fine. Just reply to any of our emails or call reception to cancel. You can come back whenever you're ready.

If you need help urgently

The counselling service isn't an emergency service. If you're in danger or can't keep yourself safe:

  • Call 999 or go to your nearest A&E.
  • Call Samaritans on 116 123, free, any time, day or night.
  • Out of hours on campus, call Campus Security on 01632 960 999.

Need help right now?

If you're in immediate danger or feel unable to keep yourself safe, please don't wait for a counselling appointment.

  • Call 999 or go to your nearest A&E.
  • Call Samaritans on 116 123, free, 24 hours a day.
  • Out of hours on campus: Campus Security, 01632 960 999.
  • During office hours you can also drop into the Wellbeing Hub and ask to speak to the duty counsellor.
